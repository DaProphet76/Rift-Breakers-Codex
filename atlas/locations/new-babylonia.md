# New Babylonia

## Summary
New Babylonia is the principal city setting established at the opening of Book One. It is organized through protected inner rings and poorer outer districts, with walls, checkpoints, rail infrastructure, labour systems, ration points, and official controls shaping everyday movement.

## Description
Chapter 1 presents New Babylonia from its eastern outer district. The visible city fabric mixes repaired housing and industrial infrastructure with ruins from older eras. High lamps burn through the night near district boundaries, watchtowers rise along the eastern wall, and rail lines connect the city to eastern settlements.

The prologue establishes a wider ring-based social structure: populations retreated inward over time, inner districts became brighter and better guarded, and outer rings were left more exposed to danger.

## Environment / Conditions
- Industrial, crowded, and visibly repaired rather than uniformly rebuilt
- Frequent fog, coal smoke, soot, machine noise, and weather exposure in the eastern districts
- Old structures from before the Sundering remain incorporated into inhabited areas
- Movement is regulated through checkpoints, permits, inspectors, and gates
- Wealth and security increase toward the better-protected rings

## Population / Control
- Populated by labourers, tradespeople, railway workers, guards, inspectors, families, and officials
- The city maintains checkpoints, armed guards, travel documentation, ration infrastructure, and defensive walls
- Exact governing structure is not yet fully established in current canon
- A city council is mentioned by Darius, confirming a civic governing body exists, but its composition and authority remain undefined

## Risks
- Unequal protection between inner and outer districts
- Restricted movement and dependence on official permits
- Corruption or bribery is feared by residents, though the exact institutional extent is not yet defined
- Raker and rift threats can breach major defensive infrastructure
- Outer residents may be trapped outside or between defensive boundaries during emergencies

## Narrative Use
- Primary urban setting for Book One
- Establishes the class divide between protected inner rings and exposed outer districts
- Provides the institutional pressure that shapes Asher, Sera, Kade, Mirella, and the Vale family
- Supplies recurring visual language: walls, rings, checkpoints, industrial rail, patched housing, old ruins, soot, warning lights, and controlled movement

## Canon Notes
- **Confirmed:** Ring-based inequality; outer districts; eastern wall; checkpoints; rail travel; travel permits; ration infrastructure; old pre-Sundering ruins; armed city security.
- **Confirmed by prologue:** Inner cities/rings are brighter and more guarded; outer rings measure life more directly by survival.
- **Intentionally unknown:** Full city map; total number and names of rings; population; exact government; exact technology level across all districts.
- **Open questions:** Relationship between the terms outer district, outer ring, and fringe in formal city geography; exact boundaries of New Babylonia beyond the eastern wall.
