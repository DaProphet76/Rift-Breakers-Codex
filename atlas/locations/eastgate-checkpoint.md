# Eastgate Checkpoint

## Summary
The Eastgate Checkpoint controls civilian passage through New Babylonia's eastern boundary toward the station and outbound rail routes. Darius Vale's travelling party must pass through it before reaching Train Seven.

## Description
Travellers queue beneath iron arches while guards in dark coats inspect papers and open luggage. Inspectors can empty and search personal goods directly on the stone ground. Beyond the checkpoint lies an inner yard leading toward the freight platform and rail facilities.

Inspector Vey is the named official who processes the Vale/Renn party. He reviews destination, purpose, occupation, permit dates, labour exemptions, dependent status, and the identities of the children before stamping the documents.

## Environment / Conditions
- Growing civilian queues near the Eastern Wall
- Iron inspection arches
- Stone ground
- Guards in dark coats
- Luggage-search area
- Bell signals used for operational changes
- Direct approach toward the station's inner yard

## Population / Control
- Inspectors and guards exercise authority over travellers and possessions
- Travel depends on valid permits and current gate status
- An inspection-change bell signal can alter checkpoint operations
- Exact agency name and command structure are not established

## Risks
- Arbitrary or invasive searches
- Residents fear bribery or changing official demands
- Gate closure can invalidate travel permits
- During emergencies, the checkpoint becomes part of the containment zone rather than a safe exit

## Narrative Use
- First direct encounter with New Babylonia's institutional control in Chapter 1
- Establishes Inspector Vey
- Provides visual contrast between civilians carrying their lives and officials controlling movement

## Canon Notes
- **Confirmed:** Iron arches; guards in dark coats; luggage searches; document inspection; Inspector Vey; inner yard beyond; operational bell signal.
- **Intentionally unknown:** Official agency name, number of lanes, checkpoint dimensions, standard staffing, formal search rules, and architecture beyond what Chapter 1 shows.
- **Open questions:** Whether Eastgate is one of several equivalent city checkpoints and how its authority relates to broader city government.
