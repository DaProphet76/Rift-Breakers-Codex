# Eastern Wall

## Summary
The Eastern Wall is a major defensive boundary of New Babylonia and the dominant landmark of Chapter 1's eastern district. It separates the outer approach and rail facilities from the better-protected interior and can be sealed during an emergency.

## Description
The wall is high enough to dominate the district skyline. Watchtowers and high lamps are mounted along the boundary. Its eastern gate is large enough to control rail and civilian passage, and iron-arched inspection lanes operate nearby.

The wall is visually imposing but socially unequal in purpose: Chapter 1 explicitly notes that it does not truly protect the outer district. Residents of the fringe live in its shadow while the wall primarily protects the city and richer rings from dangers outside or already beyond the barrier.

## Environment / Conditions
- Often partially obscured by fog in the morning
- High lamps remain visible at night along the boundary
- Rail lines, checkpoint infrastructure, gatehouse defenses, and Eastgate Station cluster around it
- A service trench passes beneath the wall toward the eastern maintenance yard

## Population / Control
- Controlled by inspectors, armed guards, and gate personnel
- Gate closure is an emergency containment measure
- Travel permits can become invalid if the gate seals before authorized departure
- Exact command hierarchy is not yet established

## Risks
- Gate closure can trap civilians on the wrong side
- Rift emergence can render the barrier ineffective
- Rail and pedestrian chokepoints concentrate crowds during emergencies
- Service routes beneath the wall create necessary but controlled secondary passages

## Narrative Use
- Visual symbol of protection that is distributed unequally
- Physical obstacle during the Train Seven crisis
- Defines the separation between New Babylonia's controlled interior and the eastern road/settlements
- Provides key animation landmarks: wall, watchtowers, lamps, gatehouse, great doors, rail line, and service trench

## Canon Notes
- **Confirmed:** Watchtowers; high lamps; great eastern doors; checkpoint; gatehouse; rail connection; emergency closure; service trench beneath the wall.
- **Confirmed social function:** Outer residents understand the wall protects those farther inside more than it protects them.
- **Intentionally unknown:** Height, thickness, construction material, full circumference, age, engineering history, and total number of gates.
- **Open questions:** Formal administrative name of the wall and whether each ring has comparable barriers.
