# Bell Signal System

## Summary
Bells are used in New Babylonia for public warnings, checkpoint operations, and rail timing. Chapter 1 establishes that residents learn distinct signals well enough for a three-year-old Asher to recognize multiple warning bells.

## Canon Notes
- **Confirmed:** Asher knows six warning-bell signals by age three.
- **Confirmed checkpoint signal:** Three slow notes followed by two quick notes indicate an inspection change at the eastern checkpoint.
- **Confirmed rail/time signal:** Train Seven is scheduled to depart at the second noon bell; the first noon bell sounds shortly before the Eastgate rift opens, and the second begins ringing during the manifestation.
- **Intentionally unknown:** The meanings of the remaining warning signals; whether noon bells are part of the same six-signal warning system; citywide bell locations and command authority.

## Details
- Bells are a routine part of life in the Outer Districts rather than rare ceremonial sounds.
- Signal patterns can communicate operational changes without direct announcement.
- Children learn the sounds because they have practical consequences for safety and movement.
- Eastgate includes a bell tower large enough that its collapse damages Train Seven and the platform during the rift attack.

## Cultural Impact
- Bell literacy is part of survival knowledge in the Outer Districts.
- Warning sounds shape childhood and reinforce the population's constant awareness of institutional or external danger.
- Mirella explicitly uses Asher knowing all six warning bells as evidence that New Babylonia is not a safe childhood environment.

## Known Conflicts / Tensions
- The system can warn or coordinate, but it cannot prevent an event such as the Eastgate rift attack.
- The distinction between civic warning bells, checkpoint codes, and timekeeping bells remains undefined.
