# New Babylonia Rail Network

## Summary
New Babylonia uses a rail network for passenger movement, freight handling, maintenance traffic, and eastbound resettlement. Chapter 1 shows the system at Eastgate Station through Train Seven, freight platforms, signaling equipment, service tracks, maintenance passages, and a smaller evacuation rail consist.

## Canon Notes
- **Confirmed:** Steam-powered or steam-associated mainline train operation; rail routes leave New Babylonia toward Eastmere; freight and passenger functions share Eastgate; maintenance/service lines exist beneath or around the city; signaling rooms use mechanical coloured levers.
- **Confirmed personnel:** Conductors, drivers, porters, guards, and maintenance/trench crews.
- **Intentionally unknown:** Formal railway authority, network map, gauge standards, total station count, ticketing organization, manufacturing base, and whether all routes use the same locomotive technology.

## Details
- Train Seven is a twelve-car eastbound resettlement train led by a squat iron engine.
- Eastgate has a freight platform, baggage handling, multiple tracks, an outer track, and maintenance infrastructure.
- A small rail engine with two freight cars and an open passenger wagon is used during the emergency evacuation.
- Narrow service lines beneath the city use smaller maintenance carriages.
- Rail travel time from Eastgate to Eastmere is estimated at two days under clear-line conditions and four days if the line is obstructed or delayed.

## Cultural Impact
- Rail transport is a major path for legally authorized movement out of New Babylonia.
- A travel chest bearing an eastern rail mark publicly signals that a household is leaving and can attract envy or attention.
- The network links civic control, labour, freight, and resettlement into the same infrastructure.

## Known Conflicts / Tensions
- Damaged track can immediately invalidate resettlement plans.
- Passenger trains become dangerous confinement spaces during emergency lockdowns.
- The rail system depends on checkpoints and gate status rather than functioning independently of city security.
