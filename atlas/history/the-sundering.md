# The Sundering

## Summary
The Sundering is a historical dividing point referenced in Chapter 1 when describing ruins in New Babylonia's oldest Outer District areas. Buildings and monuments from before the Sundering survive only in broken or repurposed form, indicating that the event lies generations in the past.

## Date / Era
- Exact date: TBD
- Relative placement: Generations before Chapter 1
- Relationship to the prologue's "Great Fall" or later wars: Not yet explicitly established in current canon

## What Happened
Chapter 1 does not explain the Sundering itself. It establishes only that structures from before it survive as ruins: smooth black pillars, broken stone figures, and partial buildings whose original purpose or subjects have been forgotten.

## Consequences
- Earlier architecture survives in fragmented form beneath or beside later construction.
- Cultural memory of some pre-Sundering monuments has been lost.
- Residents reuse the remains pragmatically as parts of homes, shops, and streets.
- The event functions as a historical boundary between the old built world and the present reconstructed city.

## What People Are Taught
Not established in Chapter 1. The prologue presents a broader taught history of world catastrophe, rebuilding, wars, rifts, and the Resonance, but it does not explicitly identify the Sundering with any one of those events.

## Canon Notes
- **Confirmed:** The term "Sundering" is used as a major historical before/after marker; pre-Sundering ruins remain in the Outer Districts; enough generations have passed for original monument identities to be forgotten.
- **Do not conflate without approval:** The Sundering, the Great Fall, later wars, and the arrival of rifts may be related, but current sources do not explicitly define them as the same event.
- **Open questions:** Cause, date, geography, relationship to the Great Fall, and historical narrative taught by New Babylonia.
